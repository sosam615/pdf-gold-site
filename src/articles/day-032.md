To redact a contract before sharing it, decide which details the reader must not see, then remove them with a real redaction tool rather than a black shape, and test the saved copy. In Gold PDF you press **R**, drag over each item and save, and the affected pages are rebuilt without the removed words.

## Quick steps
1. Read the contract's confidentiality clause to confirm you may share it at all.
2. Open the PDF in Gold PDF and use Save as (**Ctrl+Shift+S**) to make a working copy.
3. If the contract is scanned, press **Ctrl+Shift+O** to run OCR so you can search it.
4. Use **Ctrl+F** to find every occurrence of each name, amount and account number.
5. Press **R** and drag a box over each item, slightly wider than the text.
6. Check the signature blocks, initials in the margins, headers, footers and annexes.
7. Press **Ctrl+S** to save. Pages with redactions are rebuilt and metadata is cleared.
8. Reopen the file, search again and copy across the bars to confirm nothing is left.

## Why you might redact a contract before sharing it
Contracts get shared far more often than people expect. A lease goes to a mortgage adviser, an employment contract goes to an accountant or a new landlord, a supplier agreement goes to an investor in due diligence, a template goes to a colleague who wants to reuse its wording. In each case the reader needs part of the document, not all of it.

Redaction lets you send the useful part and keep the rest private. The adviser can see the rent and the term but not the landlord's bank account. The investor can see that an exclusivity clause exists without seeing the price list. The colleague can borrow the clause wording without seeing the other party's name at all.

Before redacting anything, read the confidentiality clause. Many commercial contracts limit disclosure to named people or purposes, and redaction does not automatically make sharing allowed. If the clause is strict, ask the other party, or get advice; this article covers the technical side, not the legal one.

## What to redact in a contract
Work through the contract as a reader who should not learn anything they do not need. The table lists the usual candidates.

| Item | Where it usually appears | Redact when |
|---|---|---|
| Names of individuals | Parties clause, signature blocks, notices clause | The reader has no need to know who signed |
| Signatures and initials | Last page, margins of every page | Almost always for a shared copy |
| Addresses, emails, phone numbers | Parties clause, notices clause | They belong to a private person |
| ID, tax or company numbers | Parties clause, schedules | The reader does not need to verify identity |
| Bank details | Payment clause, invoices in annexes | Always, unless the reader is paying |
| Prices, rates, discounts | Payment clause, price schedules | They are commercially sensitive |
| Side letters and special terms | Annexes, amendments | They are outside what the reader should see |
| File name and properties | Saved file | The name or title repeats a party's name |

Two places are easy to miss. The first is the notices clause near the end, which often repeats every name, address and email from the front page. The second is the annexes: price schedules, invoices and copies of ID attached after the signature page. Scroll the whole document, not just the main body.

## How to redact the contract properly in Gold PDF
Covering text is not redacting it. A black rectangle, a black highlight or a white Whiteout box only sits on top of the words, and the words stay in the file where search, copy and paste can find them. Real redaction removes them. The difference is explained in [what is the difference between redacting and whiteout](/articles/what-is-the-difference-between-redacting-and-whiteout/).

In Gold PDF the process looks like this:

1. **Work on a copy.** Save as (**Ctrl+Shift+S**) with a name such as "Lease - shared copy.pdf". Keep the original in a private folder.
2. **Make scans searchable.** Signed contracts are often scans. Press **Ctrl+Shift+O** to recognise the text; English and Hebrew are bundled. Without this step, search cannot find the names you need to remove.
3. **Find every occurrence.** Press **Ctrl+F** and search each name, amount and account number in turn. A tenant's name might appear on page 1, in the notices clause on page 9 and in an annex on page 14.
4. **Mark each item.** Press **R** and drag a box over it. Make the box a little wider than the text, so the bar does not reveal the exact length of a short name.
5. **Do not forget the margins.** Many contracts are initialled on every page. Each initial is a small signature and should be covered like one.
6. **Save.** Press **Ctrl+S**. Every page with a redaction is rebuilt as a high-resolution image with the bars burned in, the remaining words are restored as an invisible searchable layer, and the removed words are not included. Document metadata is cleared in the same save.

If many pages carry the same detail, such as a party name in every footer, the approach in [how to redact multiple pages in a PDF at once](/articles/how-to-redact-multiple-pages-in-a-pdf-at-once/) saves time.

## Keep the contract readable after redaction
A shared contract should still make sense. Over-redacting creates its own problems: the reader asks for the full version, or misreads a clause because a key word disappeared.

- **Redact values, not clauses.** Remove "$4,800 per month", not the whole payment clause.
- **Keep defined terms.** If "the Landlord" is used throughout, you only need to remove the landlord's name where it is defined, not every "the Landlord".
- **Add a short note if needed.** In the email, say what was removed: "Bank details, signatures and the price schedule have been redacted." That avoids questions and shows the gaps are deliberate.
- **Keep page numbers.** Do not delete pages from a contract you are sharing as evidence of terms; redact their content instead, so references like "see Schedule 2" still point somewhere.

Personal details deserve the same care you would give a bank statement or ID document; [how to redact bank account numbers in a PDF](/articles/how-to-redact-bank-account-numbers-in-a-pdf/) covers that part in more detail.

## Check the redacted contract before it leaves
Do not trust the screen. Close the file, reopen the saved copy, and test it:

- Search for each removed name, number and amount. The search should find nothing.
- Drag-select across a black bar, copy, and paste into Notepad. The paste should skip the redacted words.
- Click a bar. It should not move or delete, because it is part of the page, not a shape.
- Open the document properties and confirm the title and author do not name a party.
- Check bookmarks and comments, which can quote text from the contract.
- Rename the file if it contains a name, such as "Smith lease 2026.pdf".

The full checklist is in [how to check if a PDF redaction is secure](/articles/how-to-check-if-a-pdf-redaction-is-secure/).

## A short example
A freelancer is asked by a new client for an example of a previous services agreement, to agree on similar terms. The old agreement names the previous client, its director, their email and phone, the day rate and the freelancer's bank details. The new client only needs the structure and the clause wording.

The freelancer opens the agreement in Gold PDF, saves a copy, and searches the old client's name: it appears in the parties clause, the notices clause and the footer of every page. They press **R** and cover each occurrence, then cover the director's name and signature, the email and phone, the day rate in the payment clause and the bank details in the annex. They leave defined terms like "the Client" untouched. After pressing Ctrl+S they reopen the file, search for the client's name and the rate, and get no results. Copying the payment clause into Notepad gives the sentence with a gap where the rate was. The copy is ready to send.

> **Common mistakes**
> - Using black highlights or rectangles, which leave the text underneath.
> - Redacting the front page but missing the notices clause, footers or annexes.
> - Leaving initials in the page margins.
> - Redacting so much that the clauses no longer make sense.
> - Sending the file under its original name, which includes a party's name.
> - Sharing a contract whose confidentiality clause does not allow it.

## What Gold PDF does and does not do here
Gold PDF is a free PDF editor for Windows that works offline, so the contract never leaves your computer while you work on it. Its Redact tool (**R**) removes the marked content when you save by rebuilding each affected page, and it clears document metadata in the same save; OCR (**Ctrl+Shift+O**) makes scanned contracts searchable first. It does not know which details in your contract are sensitive, it has no one-click command that finds and redacts every match for you, and it gives no legal advice about whether you may share the document. It also cannot recall a copy that has already been sent. For an overview of the tool, see [Redact a PDF](/redact-pdf/).
