To delete text from a PDF, open the file in an editor that can change existing text, click the line you want to remove, clear the words and save. In Gold PDF you press **E**, click the line, delete the words and press Ctrl+S, all for free, offline and on Windows.

## Quick steps
1. Open the PDF in Gold PDF with Ctrl+O, or drag it onto the window.
2. Use **Save as** (Ctrl+Shift+S) so the original stays untouched.
3. Press Ctrl+F and search for the words to find every place they appear.
4. Press **E**. Dotted boxes appear around each line of text.
5. Click the line, select the words you want gone, press Delete and then Enter.
6. Repeat for each occurrence, then press Ctrl+S.
7. Reopen the saved file, search again, and confirm the words no longer appear.

## Why deleting text in a PDF is not like deleting in Word
A word processor stores paragraphs. Delete a sentence and everything below flows up to fill the space. A PDF stores positioned runs of characters: this word here, that line there, nothing connecting one to the next. There is no paragraph to reflow and no instruction that says "move up if something disappears".

So deleting text from a PDF has two parts. First, the words have to be removed from the page. Second, you decide what to do about the space they leave behind. Most of the confusion people have comes from expecting the second part to happen on its own.

If you still have the source document, the cleanest route is to delete there and export again. When you do not have it, which is common with documents from clients, print shops or old archives, editing the PDF directly is faster and works fine for single lines or short passages.

## How to delete a line or a few words
Gold PDF's Edit text tool works on whole lines. After you press **E**, every line on the page gets a dotted outline. Clicking one opens it with the current words filled in.

- **A few words in the middle of a line.** Select them, press Delete, and check that the remaining words still read properly. Then press Enter.
- **A whole line.** Select all the text in the line, press Delete and press Enter. The line is removed and the gap stays.
- **A word that appears many times.** Use Ctrl+F first and go through the hits one by one. Editing one occurrence does not change the others.

Zoom to 150% or more before you save. A deleted word can leave a double space or an odd-looking gap, and these are much easier to spot when enlarged.

## What happens to the gap
Because the page does not reflow, the area where the text was stays empty. You have three sensible choices:

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| One word out of a sentence | Close the gap by editing the whole line so the words sit together |
| A full line in a list or address block | Leave the white space, or move the lines below up by retyping them |
| A whole paragraph | Accept the gap, or add a short replacement sentence that fills the space |
| A line in a table | Delete the content but keep the row so columns stay aligned |

For anything where the page must look as if the text was never there, a small amount of retyping nearby is normal. Retyping is not difficult, but it is the main reason editing a PDF takes longer than editing a Word file. [How to add text to a PDF document](/articles/how-to-add-text-to-a-pdf-document/) covers placing new text in the space you freed.

## Delete text or redact it?
These two jobs sound alike but protect against different things.

- **Delete** (Edit text, **E**) removes words so the page reads the way you want. It is for corrections: an old price, a wrong name, a line that should not be there.
- **Redact** (**R**) removes content and leaves a visible bar. It is for confidential information that the reader must not see, and it shows that something was removed on purpose.

If the reason you are deleting is that the text is private, use Redact. The steps are in [how to redact a PDF so it cannot be undone](/articles/how-to-redact-a-pdf-so-it-cannot-be-undone/), and removal without any visible bar is covered in [how to permanently remove text from a PDF](/articles/how-to-permanently-remove-text-from-a-pdf/).

Also remember that a PDF can hold text in places other than the visible page, such as the document title, author and comments. If you are removing sensitive wording, search those too. See [how to remove hidden data from a PDF](/articles/how-to-remove-hidden-data-from-a-pdf/).

## Deleting text from a scanned PDF
A scan is a picture of a page, so there are no words to select. Run OCR first: press Ctrl+Shift+O, choose the language (English and Hebrew are bundled and work offline), and wait for the recognised lines to appear. Then press **E** and delete as usual.

Recognition is good but not perfect, so check the lines you plan to delete. On a poor scan, a misread character can mean the line you clicked does not match what you see. For text that must not remain visible on a scan, use Redact (**R**) rather than editing, because the original picture is still underneath a recognised text layer.

## Checking that the text is really gone
After you save, test the file rather than trusting the screen:

1. Close the document and reopen the saved copy.
2. Press Ctrl+F and search for a word you deleted.
3. Drag across the area where the text used to be, copy, and paste into Notepad.
4. Check the page again at 150% for stray fragments, extra spaces or half-deleted words.

If the search finds the word, you probably deleted a different copy of it, or the same wording exists elsewhere on the page.

> **Common mistakes**
> - Drawing a white box over the text and calling it deleted. The words are still in the file.
> - Editing the original instead of a copy, then discovering you need the old version.
> - Deleting one occurrence of a name and missing the others.
> - Leaving a double space or a stray comma behind.
> - Using delete for private information where Redact is the right tool.
> - Forgetting to save, or testing the open document instead of the saved one.

## What Gold PDF does and does not do
Gold PDF is a free, offline PDF editor for Windows. Its Edit text tool (**E**) changes existing lines, rebuilds the edited page when you save, and keeps the file on your computer. Redact (**R**) removes content for good, OCR (Ctrl+Shift+O) makes scans editable, and the interface is available in 30 languages. It does not reflow text, so deleting a paragraph leaves a gap that you tidy by hand, and it does not decide for you what is sensitive. It runs on Windows only, not on Mac, Linux or phones.
