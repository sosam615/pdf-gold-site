To redact a signature from a PDF, cover it with a true redaction tool and save, so the signature image or ink strokes are removed from the file rather than hidden under a box. In Gold PDF, press **R**, drag over the signature with a small margin, press Ctrl+S to rebuild the page, and then check the saved copy.

## Quick steps
1. Open the PDF in Gold PDF and use **Save as** (Ctrl+Shift+S) to make a working copy with a neutral file name.
2. Scroll through every page and note each handwritten signature, set of initials, stamp and signature block.
3. Press **R** and drag a box over the first signature. Make it a little larger than the ink, including any flourish that crosses printed text.
4. Repeat for initials in page corners, the typed name and title under the signature line, and the signing date if it identifies the person.
5. Press Ctrl+S. Every page with a redaction is rebuilt with the bars burned in.
6. Reopen the saved file, click on each black bar, try to drag or copy it, and zoom in to check the edges.

## Why a signature needs real redaction
A signature is one of the most sensitive things in a document. It identifies the signer, and a clean copy of it can be pasted onto another document. Yet signatures are often the part people protect worst, because they look like part of the page.

In a PDF, a signature usually exists in one of three forms:

| How the signature got there | What it is inside the file | What a covering shape leaves behind |
|---|---|---|
| Signed on paper and scanned | Part of a full-page scan image | The whole scan, including the ink, under the shape |
| Pasted from a signature tool | A separate image placed on the page | A clean, extractable copy of the signature |
| Drawn with a pen or ink tool | Vector strokes or an ink annotation | Strokes that can be selected, moved or deleted |
| Certified digital signature | A signature field with a cryptographic seal and a visible appearance | The appearance image and the signer's certificate details |

The second row is the most dangerous. A pasted signature is often a transparent PNG with no background, which is exactly what someone would need to forge it. If you only draw a black rectangle over it, anyone with a PDF editor can move the rectangle away, and many tools can export every image in a file in one step. The reasons are explained in [does drawing a black box redact a PDF](/articles/does-drawing-a-black-box-redact-a-pdf/).

## How to redact a signature step by step in Gold PDF
Start with a copy. The signed original is your record of the agreement, so keep it in a safe place and work on a second file. Press Ctrl+Shift+S and save the copy as something like "agreement-public.pdf".

Find every signature before you mark any. Signatures are not searchable text, so Ctrl+F will not find them. Scroll through the document at a zoom level where you can see whole pages and look at the last page, the corners of every page (where initials go), any witness block, and attachments or schedules at the end. Lease agreements, loan papers and employment contracts often have initials on every page.

Then press **R** to choose the Redact tool and drag over the first signature. Signatures are untidy: the loop of a "g" or a long underline can run well beyond the signature line. Draw the box generously, a few millimetres past the outermost stroke on every side. If the ink crosses printed words you need to keep, you have a choice: cover those words too, or accept a small stroke at the edge. Leaving part of a signature visible is the worse option.

Next, look at what surrounds the signature. A signature block usually has a printed name, a job title, a date and sometimes an email or phone number. These are text, so you can search for them. Press Ctrl+F, type the signer's surname and step through every result, placing a box on each. Do the same for their first name and initials.

When every box is placed, press Ctrl+S. Gold PDF rebuilds each page that contains a redaction as a sharp, high-resolution image with the bars burned in, so the signature image, the strokes and the scan pixels underneath are replaced by solid colour. The remaining words are restored as an invisible text layer, so the page stays searchable. On save, the document title and author are also cleared. Pages without redactions are left untouched.

## Signatures on scanned documents
If the document was signed on paper and scanned, the signature is part of one large picture of the page. There is no separate image to extract, but the ink is still there under any shape you draw, and a covering shape can be removed in seconds.

The Redact tool works on scans the same way: drag a box over the signature, save, and the covered pixels are replaced when the page is rebuilt. To find the printed name and title on a scan, run text recognition first with Ctrl+Shift+O, which works offline and includes English and Hebrew. Recognition will not find the handwriting itself, so check each page by eye as well. The full scan workflow is in [how to redact a scanned PDF document](/articles/how-to-redact-a-scanned-pdf-document/).

Stamps and seals need the same treatment. A company stamp with a registration number or an official seal from a notary identifies the signer as clearly as the signature does. Treat them as images and cover them as regions; the method is in [how to redact an image inside a PDF](/articles/how-to-redact-an-image-inside-a-pdf/).

## Digitally signed PDFs
A certified digital signature is different from a picture of handwriting. It is a cryptographic seal that proves the file has not changed since it was signed. Any change to the file, including a redaction, breaks that seal, and the signature panel in a PDF reader will show it as invalid or modified.

That is expected and not a fault of the redaction. If you need to share a redacted version of a digitally signed document, redact a copy and say clearly that it is a redacted copy. Keep the signed original as your proof. If a recipient needs to verify the signature, they need the original, not the redacted copy.

## Test the redacted signature
Always test the saved file, not the one you were editing. Close it and open it again, then:

1. Click on each black bar and try to drag it. On a rebuilt page it is part of the page and will not move.
2. Drag a selection across the bar and paste into Notepad. Nothing from the signature block should appear.
3. Zoom to 400% and look at the edges of each bar for stray tails of ink.
4. Search for the signer's name, initials and email.
5. Open the comments panel. If the signature was added as an ink comment, make sure it is gone there too.
6. Open the document properties and check the title, author and subject.

If anything is wrong, go back to the original and make a fresh copy rather than patching the tested file. The full checklist is in [how to check if a PDF redaction is secure](/articles/how-to-check-if-a-pdf-redaction-is-secure/).

> **Common mistakes**
> - Drawing a black rectangle or using Whiteout over a pasted signature, which leaves a clean copy in the file.
> - Making the box too tight and leaving a flourish or underline visible.
> - Removing the main signature but missing initials on other pages or a witness signature.
> - Leaving the printed name, title and email below the signature line.
> - Redacting the only copy of a signed contract.

## What Gold PDF does and does not do
Gold PDF is a free Windows application that works offline, needs no account and never uploads your file. Its Redact tool removes covered images, ink strokes and scan pixels from every page it rebuilds on save, and it clears the title and author at the same time. It does not find signatures for you: handwriting is not text, so you must locate each signature yourself. It does not preserve a digital signature after a change, because no tool can. Plain Whiteout only covers content and is not a redaction. Gold PDF also has a signatures library for adding your own signature to documents, which is the opposite job; it is described in [how to sign a PDF without printing](/articles/how-to-sign-a-pdf-without-printing/).
