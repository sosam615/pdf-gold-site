How to Edit Text in a Scanned PDF

Updated 2026-11-20 · 6 min read · by Gold PDF

To edit text in a scanned PDF, first run OCR so the picture of the page becomes real text, then use an edit tool to change it. In Gold PDF that is Ctrl+Shift+O to recognise the text, E to click a line and type the new wording, and Ctrl+S to save, all offline and for free on Windows.

Quick steps

  1. Open the scan with Ctrl+O and use Save as (Ctrl+Shift+S) at once, so the original scan stays untouched.
  2. Press Ctrl+Shift+O, choose the document language (English or Hebrew) and wait while the pages are recognised.
  3. Zoom in and compare the recognised words with the picture, especially names, dates, amounts and email addresses.
  4. Press E. Boxes appear around the lines that now count as text.
  5. Click the line you want to change, edit only the characters that differ and press Enter.
  6. Check the page at 150% zoom for alignment, size and colour.
  7. Save with Ctrl+S, reopen the file and search with Ctrl+F for the old words.

Why a scanned PDF cannot be edited straight away

A scanner or a phone camera produces a photograph of a page. The PDF that holds it contains an image, not letters. There is nothing for an edit tool to click on, which is why the pointer does nothing when you try to select a sentence.

OCR, short for optical character recognition, looks at the picture and works out which letters it shows. It then adds an invisible layer of text on top of the image, lined up with the printed words. After that the page behaves much more like a normal document: you can search it, copy from it and select lines. Our guide to how to make a scanned PDF searchable covers that first step in more detail, and how to OCR a scanned PDF for free gives a full walkthrough.

It helps to be clear about what OCR does not do. It does not clean up the picture, and it does not make the original scan disappear. The page still displays the original image, with the recognised text sitting invisibly above it. That detail explains most of the surprises people meet when they edit a scan.

How to check that OCR read the page correctly

Recognition is good on clean, straight, printed pages and weaker on small print, stamps, handwriting, curved pages and low-resolution photos. A single wrong character can turn a number such as 1,860 into 1,880, and the result still looks like a number.

Before you change anything, spend a few minutes on a short review:

What to checkWhy it matters
Names and surnamesUnusual spellings are the most common recognition error
Numbers, dates and amountsA wrong digit changes the meaning without looking wrong
Email addresses and web linksOne wrong letter makes the address useless
Lines near stamps, folds or shadowsRecognition is weakest where the picture is dirty
Pages that were photographed at an angleLines can be read in the wrong order

If many words are wrong, improve the source rather than the text. A rescan at 300 dpi, a straighter page and better light usually do more than hours of correction.

How to edit the recognised words

Once the text is right, press E. Click the line you want to change and type. If a single word is wrong, select only that word instead of retyping the whole line, because the fewer characters you touch, the less can shift.

Two kinds of edit behave differently on a scan:

  • Correcting the recognised text. If OCR read "Invoce" instead of "Invoice", fixing it repairs the searchable layer. The picture still shows what was printed, but a search now finds the right word. This is the easy case, and nothing about the page's look changes.
  • Changing what the page says. If you want the page to show a different amount or date, the printed picture still contains the old value. Your new text is drawn at that position, so the old printed characters must not remain visible beneath or beside it.

For the second case, work in two steps. Press R and redact the old words, so the pixels are replaced when you save, and then place the new text in the cleared spot. This is more reliable than covering the old value with a white box, which only sits on top of the image. For the general method on a page made of text, see how to correct a typo in a PDF, and for a similar job on a different document, how to change a date in a PDF.

What the edited scan will and will not look like

Be realistic about the result. A scanned page has noise, a slightly off-white background and a font that you do not have installed. New text is crisp and perfectly aligned, so it can stand out against the grain around it.

You can reduce the contrast between the old and the new in a few ways. Match the text size to the surrounding lines by zooming in and comparing. Keep the colour dark grey if the original print is not true black. Keep the new text about the same length as the old, so it does not run into a neighbour. If the page will be printed or sent to someone who inspects it closely, look at it on screen at 150% before you save.

Also remember what you are changing. A scanned contract, certificate, receipt or official letter is often a record of what someone signed or received. Editing the wording of a document in order to mislead anyone is wrong, and in many places it is illegal. Legitimate uses are fixing your own mistakes, updating your own details, preparing a draft or removing private information before you share a copy.

Common mistakes

- Skipping OCR and expecting to click on text that is only a picture.- Trusting the recognised words without comparing them with the image.- Covering the old value with a white rectangle. The old pixels are still in the file.- Editing the only copy instead of using Save as first.- Choosing the wrong OCR language, which produces nonsense words.- Making the new line much longer than the old one, so it overlaps its neighbours.- Forgetting to reopen the saved file and search for the old words.

When to rescan instead of editing

Sometimes the quickest fix is not an edit at all. If the page is crooked, too dark or cut off at the edge, take a new scan. If most of the document needs to change, retype it in a word processor and save a new PDF. If you only need to hide private information on the scan, you do not need to edit anything: see how to redact a scanned PDF document, which explains how to remove information from the image itself.

A good rule is to edit when fewer than a handful of lines change, and rebuild when the changes run across pages.

Hebrew and mixed-language scans

OCR needs to know what language it is reading. Gold PDF bundles English and Hebrew, so a Hebrew scan can be recognised offline without adding anything. For a page that mixes the two, such as a Hebrew letter with English product names, run the recognition and then review the English words slowly, since mixed pages are where errors cluster. After editing a right-to-left line, check the order of the words and the position of punctuation, which can shift to the wrong end of a line.

Frequently asked questions

Can I edit text in a scanned PDF?

Yes, after OCR. A scan is a picture, so run OCR (Ctrl+Shift+O in Gold PDF) to create real text, then press E, click a line and type the new wording.

Will the edited scan look identical to the original page?

Not exactly. The page image stays as it was, and your edit is drawn as new text. Check the result at 150% zoom, because the background and font can differ slightly from the scanned original.

Does OCR work offline?

In Gold PDF, yes. English and Hebrew are bundled, so the scan never leaves your computer.

Why does the text I edit still show the old words when I select it?

The old words can remain in the scanned image or in the OCR text layer. If they must be gone, use Redact (R) on that area and then type the replacement.

How do I fix a word that OCR got wrong?

Press E, click the line, correct the word and save. Always compare names, numbers and email addresses with the image, because a recognition error often looks like a normal word.
